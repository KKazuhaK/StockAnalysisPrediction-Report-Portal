# v0.4.46 — the chart gets its own app, and stops being A-share only

## Upgrade notes

From v0.4.45 — pull and restart. No schema change, no migration. Everything below either stores
nothing or ships with a default that reproduces exactly what v0.4.45 already did.

**The reading page looks different, deliberately.** The quote chart is collapsed when you open a
report. Expanding it is one click and the choice is remembered per browser, so if you want it every
time you set that once. The full-size chart moved to a new app — 应用 → 行情.

**The portal now reaches two more vendor paths** when somebody opens the quotes app on a Hong Kong or
US symbol. Same two hosts as before (`qt.gtimg.cn`, `web.ifzq.gtimg.cn`); no new destination.

Rolling back to v0.4.45 restores the old reading page and removes the app and the admin panel. The
three new settings are ordinary `meta` rows an older build ignores.

## Fixed

**The chart took over the reading page, and the wider your monitor the worse it got.** This was not a
styling nit. `PriceChart` drew into a fixed `720×300` viewBox at `height: auto`, so its aspect ratio
was pinned and its height was a *function of the window width*: 450px at 1080, 600px at 1440, and
**792px on a 1900px-wide screen** — which is where the report went below the fold. The chart now
measures its container and draws in real pixels, so its height is exactly the number of pixels asked
for at any width.

Sizing everything from a constant 720 hid a second bug: the left gutter was 48 units, which is 127
real pixels on a wide screen and *clips* `1,234,567.89` on a narrow one. It is now sized from the
widest label the chart actually prints.

**Hovering a candle appeared to do nothing.** The readout existed — as a strip underneath the SVG, so
at 792px tall it was off the bottom of the screen. There is now a floating card at the pointer with
the bar's date, open, high, low, close, change, percentage and volume, a crosshair with a price chip
on the Y axis and a date chip on the X axis, and the same card for keyboard selection. It flips sides
near the right edge so it is never clipped and never sits under the cursor.

**A timezone-less US quote read as this afternoon.** The server had been sending the exchange's zone
since this release's own backend work; nothing rendered it. So an AAPL close showed as
`行情时间 2026-09-04 16:00:01` in exactly the furniture a Shanghai close uses — twelve hours early for
a reader in China, thirteen in January. The strip now labels the zone. It still shows the exchange's
own wall clock rather than converting into the browser's zone, because converting is what destroys
the information.

**A cache TTL had a floor and no ceiling.** `2000000000` seconds is a perfectly valid `Duration`
(2e18ns, under the int64 wrap), so nothing caught it, and it would have pinned every symbol in the
cache for about sixty-three years — a frozen price with only the 缓存 chip as a hint. Clamped now to
a day, on save *and* on read, the same two places the floor was already enforced.

**Turning every quote source off reported success and turned them both back on.** The page sent an
empty order, the server read blank as "reset to the shipped default", and the switches flipped back
while a success toast was on screen. It refuses the action now, with a reason, rather than reverting
it silently.

## New

**应用 → 行情.** A first-party app beside 批量执行 and 计划任务: type a code, get the price and a
full-size chart. This is what makes the reading page's small one fair — filling the screen with a
chart is correct *here*. It is open to every signed-in user, because it reads public market data and
nothing about this portal.

**Hong Kong, the US, and indices.** `600519`, `00700` and `AAPL` all work, as do index codes. The
same two endpoints serve all of them, but almost nothing else is the same, and each difference was a
trap worth naming:

- Volume is 手 for A-shares and **already shares** for Hong Kong and the US. Applying the ×100 lot
  conversion to AAPL reports about four billion shares a day.
- The US echoes its code as `AAPL.OQ`, not `AAPL`. Left alone, the drift gate would have refused the
  entire US market.
- Timestamps come in three formats across four markets, in two timezones.
- `qt` array lengths are 88 / 87 / 78 / **71**. v0.4.45's decision to make that check a *floor*
  rather than an equality against 88 is the only reason the US parses at all.
- **The US has no usable daily series**: a 60-bar request returns two rows, 2011-06-02 and
  2026-09-04. So the US gets a price and no chart, for the same reason Beijing does — a line through
  two points fifteen years apart labelled 3个月 is worse than saying there is no history.

**Reports are still A-share only.** The stock table, the name fetch, ingest and search are unchanged.
A US ticker has a price here and no reports anywhere, and the app says so.

**管理 → 行情源.** Per source: primary or fallback, the markets it serves, last success, last error
and when, and the consecutive-failure streak — all of which the cache had been recording since
v0.4.45 and showing nowhere. Plus cache occupancy and a clear button. You can change the order, which
sources are enabled, and the two cache TTLs.

You cannot type a URL, and the page says why: a source here is not an address, it is a **parser**,
reading the vendor by fixed field positions behind a drift gate. Pointing it at another host produces
a gate refusal, not another source.

Clearing the cache deliberately leaves the health counters alone. They are the record of what the
vendors have been doing, not cached data — an operator who clears the cache to test a flapping source
would otherwise erase the evidence they pressed the button to read.

## Verification

Go build, vet, gofmt and the whole `internal/app` suite including under `-race`. Web typecheck, **644
tests across 87 files**, locale parity and the SPA build.

Two adversarial reviews ran over this before it shipped and applied **37 mutations between them**,
of which 25 were caught by the tests as written. The eleven that were not are now covered, each
verified by putting the mutation back and watching a named test fail — among them the promise that
health counters survive a cache clear, the error-code allowlist that stops an untranslated key
reaching the screen, and the guard that keeps a stale hover index from crashing the reading page when
the series shrinks under it.

The reviews also caught two things that were wrong in the *specification* rather than the code: the
working table said the US had daily history when the fixture says two rows, and the response type
declared `kind` as a two-value union when the Sina fallback legitimately sends neither value. Both
were corrected in favour of what the fixtures actually show. A route added without a tab title was
caught by the guard v0.4.45 added for exactly that, which is the second time that guard has paid for
itself.
